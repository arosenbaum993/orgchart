Curriculum Director

Position Summary

The Curriculum Director serves as the district's chief instructional officer, responsible for designing, implementing, and monitoring a coherent, standards-aligned instructional program across all three campuses. This role leads the academic coaching team, oversees the Multi-Tiered System of Supports framework, and coordinates district accountability functions.

Essential Duties and Responsibilities
Curriculum and Instruction
  • Lead the development, alignment, and implementation of a guaranteed and viable curriculum across all grade levels and content areas.
  • Direct the district's backwards design process: teachers develop the unit assessment before lessons are planned, then submit assessment and unit plan together for review before instruction begins.
  • Oversee the Unit Planning Dashboard as the primary tool for monitoring teacher-level implementation.
  • Calibrate Visible Learning expectations consistently across all three campuses.
  • Enforce the co-teaching unit submission standard: a co-taught unit plan without embedded Specially Designed Instruction is an incomplete submission.
  • Lead district-wide Test Talks and PLC structures focused on grade-level goals as shared team responsibility.
  • Oversee the selection, adoption, and fidelity of core instructional materials aligned to Georgia Standards of Excellence.
Academic Coaching and Leadership
  • Directly supervise and support Academic Coaches at all three campuses.
  • Build coaching capacity by modeling instructional practice and co-facilitating PLCs.
  • Coordinate with building principals to align coaching priorities to school-level goals.
MTSS and Student Support
  • Oversee the district's MTSS framework, ensuring fidelity of Tier I instruction and coordinated Tier II and III interventions.
  • Supervise the MTSS Coordinator and provide direction on screening, intervention data, and progress monitoring.
  • Collaborate with Special Education and Federal Programs to support identified and at-risk students.
Accountability and Data Systems
District Monitoring Role: The Curriculum Director executes the district's monitoring function over building principals, leading monthly instructional reviews focused on evidence, actions, and outcomes.
  • Execute the district's monitoring role over building principals and lead monthly principal instructional reviews.
  • Maintain Year One focus on the five instructional priorities.
  • Oversee district accountability functions including Georgia Milestones data analysis and continuous improvement planning.
  • Lead the disaggregation and interpretation of student performance data across campuses and subgroups.
  • Support the Superintendent in board reports and state and federal accountability submissions.
Professional Learning and Culture
  • Design and deliver district-wide professional development aligned to instructional priorities.
  • Foster a culture of collaborative inquiry, high expectations, and continuous improvement.
  • Serve as a thought partner to principals, academic coaches, and the Superintendent.
Qualifications
Required
  • Valid Georgia PSC certificate in a leadership or curriculum-related field
  • Master's degree in education, curriculum, or a related field
  • Minimum five years of successful classroom teaching experience
  • Minimum three years of instructional leadership experience
  • Demonstrated knowledge of GSE and the Georgia Milestones Assessment System
  • Experience leading PLC structures and data-driven instructional improvement
Preferred
  • Specialist or doctoral degree in curriculum, instruction, or educational leadership
  • Experience with MTSS frameworks and tiered intervention systems
  • Familiarity with Visible Learning research
  • Background in curriculum mapping, backwards design, and instructional coaching
  • Prior experience in a rural or small district central office
  • Experience with GaDOE accountability systems, CCRPI, and federal compliance
Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities
  • Deep knowledge of standards-based curriculum design and instructional best practices
  • Ability to analyze and interpret complex student performance data
  • Strong written and verbal communication skills for diverse audiences
  • Capacity to lead adult learners through change and professional growth
  • Skill in building collaborative relationships with principals, teachers, and cabinet peers
  • Organizational ability to manage multiple priorities simultaneously
  • Commitment to equity, high expectations, and student-centered decision-making
  • Integrity and discretion in handling sensitive personnel and student matters
Working Conditions
  • Full-time, 12-month position based at the central office with regular travel to all three campuses.
  • Attendance at evening board meetings, community events, and professional conferences required.
  • Physical demands consistent with a typical school and office environment.
  • Performance evaluated annually by the Superintendent using district leadership evaluation criteria.

Position Summary

The Director of Operations is a newly established cabinet-level position responsible for the management and coordination of all non-instructional operational functions across McIntosh County School System, including student services, nutrition, transportation, facilities and maintenance, and human resources. The Director of Operations also serves as the district's designated Title IX Coordinator.

Essential Duties and Responsibilities
Operational Leadership
  • Provide unified oversight of all operational departments with clear expectations and accountability structures.
  • Build systems that identify and address operational concerns before they escalate.
  • Serve as the primary operational point of contact for the Superintendent.
  • Coordinate operational planning across departments so campuses receive consistent support.
  • Monitor compliance with applicable federal, state, and local regulations across all operational areas.
Student Services
  • Oversee the Student Services Coordinator, including alternative school placement, tribunal coordination, Pre-K, and testing processes.
  • Ensure student services operations align with district policy and due process requirements.
Nutrition
  • Oversee the nutrition program, including USDA and GaDOE child nutrition compliance and program integrity.
  • Support the Nutrition Director in managing campus leads, staffing, and menu planning.
  • Monitor meal participation and ensure equitable access to nutrition services.
Transportation
  • Oversee route management, driver hiring and training, vehicle maintenance, and GaDOE transportation compliance.
  • Support the Transportation Director in daily operations and safety protocols.
  • Ensure transportation is delivered safely, efficiently, and in alignment with scheduling needs.
Facilities and Maintenance
  • Oversee facilities and maintenance across all three campuses to ensure safe, clean learning environments.
  • Supervise the Maintenance Director and staff, managing work orders and preventive maintenance.
  • Coordinate on capital improvement, ESPLOST implementation, and Local Facilities Plan obligations.
  • Ensure compliance with fire safety, health, accessibility, and building code requirements.
Human Resources
  • Oversee HR functions with half-time support from Viette Hunter, whose role is shared with Finance.
  • Direct onboarding, records management, certification tracking, leave administration, and policy compliance.
  • Oversee student information system functions managed by Elsie Lane.
  • Support the Superintendent in personnel processes and hiring coordination.
  • Serve as a resource on HR policy, employee relations, and Georgia PSC certification.
Title IX Coordination
Designated Title IX Coordinator: The Director of Operations serves as the district's Title IX Coordinator under 34 C.F.R. Part 106, a formal legal designation carrying specific compliance obligations.
  • Serve as the designated Title IX Coordinator across all programs, activities, and employment practices.
  • Receive, track, and respond to all Title IX complaints involving students or employees.
  • Coordinate and oversee Title IX investigations under the district's adopted grievance procedures.
  • Develop, deliver, and maintain records of annual Title IX training.
  • Review and update Title IX policies, notices, and procedures for ongoing compliance.
  • Maintain required records and ensure the Title IX notice of nondiscrimination is posted.
  • Serve as the primary district contact for GaDOE and OCR on Title IX matters.
Qualifications
Required
  • Bachelor's degree in education, business administration, public administration, or a related field
  • Minimum five years of progressive leadership in school district operations or a related public sector field
  • Demonstrated experience managing multiple operational departments simultaneously
  • Knowledge of federal and state regulations applicable to school district operations
  • Experience with Title IX compliance or willingness to complete required training upon hire
  • Strong organizational, communication, and problem-solving skills
Preferred
  • Master's degree in educational leadership, business administration, or a related field
  • Valid Georgia PSC certificate in educational leadership or a related area
  • Prior experience as a Title IX Coordinator or deputy coordinator
  • Experience with ESPLOST project management and Local Facilities Plan compliance
  • Familiarity with GaDOE reporting systems
  • Background in human resources administration in a public school or government setting
Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities
  • Broad knowledge of facilities, transportation, nutrition, and HR operational systems
  • Understanding of Title IX law, regulations, and grievance procedures
  • Ability to build reliable systems that reduce reactive problem-solving
  • Skill in supervising and developing department-level directors and staff
  • Strong communication skills for staff, families, and regulatory agencies
  • Ability to manage budgets, contracts, and vendor relationships across departments
  • Discretion and sound judgment in handling sensitive personnel, legal, and student matters
  • Commitment to safe, equitable, well-functioning environments for all students and staff
Working Conditions
  • Full-time, 12-month position based at the central office with regular travel to campuses and operational sites.
  • On-call availability required for facilities emergencies and other urgent operational matters.
  • Attendance at evening board meetings, community events, and required training.
  • Physical demands include site visits and work in both office and field environments.
  • Performance evaluated annually by the Superintendent.

Position Summary

The Special Education Director provides district-wide leadership for all programs serving students with disabilities, gifted students, and the nursing program, ensuring compliance with IDEA, Section 504, and GaDOE requirements. This role partners closely with the Curriculum Director on co-teaching, Specially Designed Instruction, and MTSS implementation.

Essential Duties and Responsibilities
Special Education Program Leadership
  • Provide district-wide leadership ensuring a free appropriate public education in the least restrictive environment.
  • Oversee the development, implementation, and annual review of IEPs across all campuses.
  • Supervise and support special education teachers and paraprofessionals.
  • Ensure a student's IEP defines the pathway to grade-level standards, not an exemption from them.
  • Coordinate eligibility determinations, evaluation timelines, and procedural safeguards under IDEA.
Co-Teaching and Specially Designed Instruction
  • Partner with the Curriculum Director to implement the co-teaching model with fidelity.
  • Ensure SDI is embedded within co-taught unit plans before district review.
  • Monitor co-taught classrooms for active engagement of both teachers and delivery of small-group intervention.
  • Develop professional learning on co-teaching, SDI strategies, and progress monitoring.
MTSS Collaboration
  • Collaborate with the Curriculum Director and MTSS Coordinator so special education and MTSS operate in coordination.
  • Guide school MTSS teams on the distinction between Tier II and Tier III and the role of evaluation.
Compliance and Federal Reporting
Federal Compliance Obligation: This role carries direct responsibility for IDEA compliance, including Child Find, evaluation timelines, IEP safeguards, and GaDOE special education reporting.
  • Ensure district-wide compliance with IDEA, Section 504, and GaDOE special education regulations.
  • Oversee required GaDOE special education data submissions.
  • Maintain accurate special education records in compliance with FERPA.
  • Coordinate with legal counsel and the Superintendent on due process and placement disputes.
  • Oversee Section 504 plans and processes district-wide.
Gifted Education
  • Oversee the gifted program to meet GaDOE identification and service requirements.
  • Monitor gifted implementation, including teacher endorsement requirements.
  • Coordinate gifted referral and evaluation for timely, equitable identification.
School Nursing and Student Health
  • Provide administrative oversight of the nursing program in compliance with Georgia school health regulations.
  • Coordinate health-related supports and integrate medical needs into IEP and 504 documentation.
  • Serve as point of contact for communicable disease, medication, and emergency health protocols.
Administrative Support
  • Direct Tina Thomas, who serves as SpEd administrative assistant on a dual-role basis with Technology.
  • Oversee Stephanie Largent, School Psychologist, including caseload and timeline compliance.
Qualifications
Required
  • Valid Georgia PSC certificate in Special Education or Educational Leadership
  • Master's degree in special education, educational leadership, or a related field
  • Minimum five years of experience in special education
  • Demonstrated knowledge of IDEA, Section 504, Child Find, and IEP development
  • Experience supervising special education staff across multiple sites
  • Knowledge of co-teaching models and Specially Designed Instruction
Preferred
  • Specialist or doctoral degree in special education or educational leadership
  • Prior experience as a district-level special education director
  • Experience with GaDOE special education data systems and federal reporting
  • Background in gifted education program administration
  • Familiarity with MTSS and the intersection of intervention and eligibility
  • Experience managing due process or compliance monitoring reviews
Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities
  • Deep knowledge of federal and state special education law and safeguards
  • Understanding of co-teaching, SDI, and integration with general education
  • Ability to maintain compliant IEP and 504 processes across campuses
  • Skill in supervising special education teachers, paraprofessionals, and related staff
  • Strong collaborative skills with the Curriculum Director, principals, and cabinet peers
  • Ability to communicate clearly with families, advocates, and legal representatives
  • Commitment to access, equity, and high expectations for every student with a disability
  • Discretion and sound judgment in due process, personnel, and confidential matters
Working Conditions
  • Full-time, 12-month position based at the central office with regular travel to campuses.
  • Attendance at IEP meetings, eligibility conferences, due process proceedings, and GaDOE reviews.
  • Attendance at evening board meetings and required professional conferences.
  • Physical demands consistent with a typical school and office environment.
  • Performance evaluated annually by the Superintendent.

Position Summary

The Federal Programs Director is a 49% position responsible for the planning, implementation, monitoring, and compliance of all federally funded programs in McIntosh County School System. This role manages the district's Consolidated Application and oversees Title I, Title II, Title III, Title IV, Migrant Education, and Homeless/McKinney-Vento programs, ensuring that federal funds are used in accordance with Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) requirements and all applicable GaDOE and U.S. Department of Education guidelines. A central responsibility in the 2026-2027 school year is the active training and preparation of Leslie Poppell, Student Services Coordinator, who is the district's designated successor for this role upon Ms. Noble's retirement. The district is scheduled for federal program monitoring during this period, and a primary objective of this position is ensuring that Ms. Poppell is fully prepared to lead the district through and beyond that monitoring cycle. Ms. Noble works two days per week, and those two days are structured to maximize direct knowledge transfer and hands-on training with Ms. Poppell.

Essential Duties and Responsibilities
Succession Training and Leadership Transition
Priority Responsibility for 2026-2027: The single most important responsibility of this position this year is the active preparation of Leslie Poppell to serve as the district's next Federal Programs Director. Ms. Noble's two working days per week are to be structured around this objective above all others.
  • Provide direct, structured training to Leslie Poppell during both days worked each week, ensuring that every area of federal program administration — Consolidated Application management, Title program compliance, fiscal reporting, GaDOE monitoring preparation, and program documentation — is covered systematically and completely before the end of the school year.
  • Develop and maintain a written transition plan documenting all critical processes, deadlines, GaDOE contacts, system access requirements, and institutional knowledge needed for Ms. Poppell to independently assume the Federal Programs Director role upon Ms. Noble's retirement.
  • Prepare Ms. Poppell specifically for the district's upcoming GaDOE federal program monitoring, ensuring she understands the monitoring process, knows what documentation will be requested, and can respond confidently and accurately to GaDOE program staff.
  • Walk Ms. Poppell through every active federal program, the current Consolidated Application, all open compliance obligations, and the status of each grant, so that no program knowledge exists only in Ms. Noble's institutional memory at the time of transition.
  • Identify and address gaps in Ms. Poppell's federal program knowledge throughout the year, adjusting training priorities based on what the monitoring schedule and program calendar demand.
  • Coordinate with the Superintendent and Director of Operations on the transition timeline to ensure that the handoff is complete, documented, and supported before Ms. Noble's retirement date.
GaDOE Monitoring Preparation
Monitoring Year: The district is scheduled for GaDOE federal program monitoring during the 2026-2027 school year. Preparing the district's documentation, processes, and staff for a successful monitoring review is a primary responsibility of this position this year.
  • Conduct a comprehensive internal review of all federal program documentation, fiscal records, program plans, and compliance evidence to identify any gaps that must be addressed before GaDOE monitoring begins.
  • Ensure that all required program documentation is complete, organized, and readily accessible, including Consolidated Application approvals, budget amendments, expenditure records, needs assessments, parent engagement documentation, and program outcome data.
  • Prepare school-based staff and administrators for monitoring interviews and documentation requests, ensuring that principals, teachers, and support staff understand what GaDOE monitors may ask and what records must be available at the campus level.
  • Coordinate with the CFO to ensure that fiscal records, expenditure documentation, and procurement records for all federal programs are complete and compliant with federal cost principles and ESSA fiscal requirements.
  • Respond to all pre-monitoring information requests from GaDOE promptly and completely, and serve as the primary district contact throughout the monitoring process.
  • Ensure that any findings from the monitoring review are understood by Ms. Poppell and that corrective action plans, if required, are developed, submitted, and implemented before Ms. Noble's departure.
Federal Grant Management and Compliance
  • Manage the district's Consolidated Application process, including annual planning, submission, revision, and approval through the GaDOE grants management system.
  • Ensure all federal programs are implemented in full compliance with ESSA, applicable federal regulations, GaDOE program guidelines, and district board policy.
  • Maintain complete and accurate documentation for all federal programs, including budget records, expenditure reports, program plans, needs assessments, and required certifications.
  • Monitor federal program budgets in coordination with the Chief Financial Officer, ensuring expenditures are allowable, allocable, and consistent with federal cost principles.
  • Ensure all required stakeholder consultation processes are completed prior to Consolidated Application submission, including private school consultation where applicable.
Title Programs (I, II, III, IV)
  • Title I: Oversee the district's Title I program, ensuring funds supplement and do not supplant state and local resources, and that services target students with the greatest academic need. Ensure required parent and family engagement activities, policies, and school-parent compacts are documented at each Title I campus.
  • Title II: Oversee professional development funding ensuring alignment to the district's instructional framework and identified staff needs.
  • Title III: Oversee services for English Learners, ensuring identification, placement, and progress monitoring comply with federal and state requirements, and that required parent notification and annual assessment requirements are met.
  • Title IV: Oversee funding for a well-rounded education, safe and healthy students, and effective use of technology in accordance with ESSA requirements and statutory spending requirements.
Migrant Education, Homeless Services, and Family Engagement
  • Administer the district's Migrant Education Program (MEP), including identification and recruitment of eligible migrant students, service coordination, and required data submission to GaDOE.
  • Serve as or designate the district's McKinney-Vento homeless education liaison, ensuring students experiencing homelessness are promptly identified, enrolled, and provided full access to district programs.
  • Oversee the work of Parent Resource Coordinator Corbin Poppell, ensuring parent and family engagement activities are coordinated, documented, and connected to student learning.
  • Ensure each Title I campus has a current school-parent compact and building-level family engagement plan.
Qualifications
Required
  • Valid Georgia PSC certificate in educational leadership or a related field
  • Bachelor's degree in education, public administration, or a related field
  • Demonstrated knowledge of ESSA, federal grant compliance, and GaDOE federal program guidelines
  • Experience with federal budget management, allowability standards, and grant reporting
  • Strong organizational skills and attention to detail in compliance record-keeping
  • Ability to train, mentor, and transfer institutional knowledge to a designated successor
Preferred
  • Master's degree in educational leadership, public administration, or a related field
  • Prior experience as a district-level federal programs coordinator or director
  • Familiarity with GaDOE's grants management system and Consolidated Application process
  • Experience with GaDOE federal program monitoring and the administrative review process
  • Background in parent and family engagement program design and implementation
  • Experience coordinating MEP and McKinney-Vento requirements
Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities
  • Thorough knowledge of ESSA, federal education program requirements, and GaDOE compliance frameworks
  • Understanding of federal cost principles, allowability standards, and supplement-not-supplant requirements
  • Ability to organize and present federal program documentation to withstand a GaDOE monitoring review
  • Skill in structured knowledge transfer and succession training in a complex compliance environment
  • Strong written and verbal communication skills for grant applications, compliance reports, and family engagement materials
  • Ability to build the capacity of a successor through deliberate, organized, and comprehensive training
  • Commitment to ensuring program continuity and district compliance beyond the term of this position
  • Discretion and sound judgment in managing sensitive student data, financial records, and compliance matters
Working Conditions
  • Part-time position at 49% FTE, working two days per week, based at the central office.
  • Both working days are to be structured around direct training and knowledge transfer with the designated successor, Leslie Poppell.
  • Peak responsibilities include Consolidated Application cycles, GaDOE monitoring preparation, and federal reporting deadlines.
  • Position is 49% funded through federal program allocations administered by this role.
  • Performance evaluated by the Superintendent in coordination with program transition objectives.

The Technology Director is responsible for the planning, implementation, maintenance, and security of all technology infrastructure across the district, including network, devices, cybersecurity, physical security systems, Google Workspace, VoIP, and the student information system. This role also carries forward-facing responsibility for enabling safe, appropriate AI access for staff.

Network Infrastructure and Systems
  • Plan, implement, and maintain network infrastructure across all three campuses.
  • Monitor network performance and manage ISP and vendor relationships.
  • Oversee technology procurement, inventory, and lifecycle planning.
  • Coordinate with the CFO on technology budgeting and ESPLOST-funded projects.
Device Management
  • Manage device deployment, configuration, and maintenance for students and staff.
  • Administer the MDM platform with content filtering and security policies.
  • Maintain a device refresh cycle and repair process that minimizes downtime.
  • Coordinate device distribution and collection with accurate assignment records.
Google Workspace Administration
  • Serve as primary Google Workspace for Education administrator.
  • Configure policies aligned with appropriate use and FERPA compliance.
  • Evaluate third-party app integrations and data sharing agreements before approval.
  • Provide training and support on Workspace tools.
VoIP, SIS, and Cybersecurity

  • Oversee the student information system and its state and federal data integrations.
  • Maintain the district cybersecurity posture, incident response plan, and staff awareness training.
  • Monitor for threats and respond promptly to incidents.
Physical Security Systems and Camera Infrastructure
Physical Security Responsibility: The Technology Director holds direct responsibility for camera infrastructure and all physical security technology, a critical component of student and staff safety to be maintained with the same urgency as any life-safety system.
  • Oversee security camera systems across all campuses, including installation, monitoring, and storage.
  • Audit camera coverage and treat outages as urgent safety matters requiring immediate resolution.
  • Manage footage storage, retention, and access controls in compliance with policy and law.
  • Oversee door access control, key card systems, alarms, and the Centegix staff alert system.
  • Oversee intercom and PA systems as core components of daily school safety.
  • Coordinate with principals and the Director of Operations on placement, drills, and upgrades.
  • Maintain a capital replacement plan so the district is never on end-of-life safety infrastructure.
Artificial Intelligence — Enabling Safe Access
District Expectation: The Technology Director is expected to be a proactive champion for responsible AI use. The role is to remove barriers to AI access for administrators and teachers, not to create them.
  • Proactively research, evaluate, and recommend AI tools that support administrators and teachers.
  • Establish an efficient, accessible process for staff to request evaluation of new AI tools.
  • Maintain district guidelines for safe and appropriate AI use, including data privacy standards.
  • Evaluate AI tools for FERPA, COPPA, and student data privacy compliance before adoption.
  • Stay current on AI developments relevant to K-12 and bring findings to the cabinet proactively.
  • Coordinate with the Curriculum Director on AI tools with instructional applications.
  • Provide AI literacy training on available tools and district boundaries.
Supervision and Data Privacy
  • Supervise campus tech support staff with clear service standards and response times.
  • Direct Tina Thomas, who provides administrative support on a dual-role basis with Special Education.
  • Serve as primary point of responsibility for technology-related data privacy compliance.
  • Review and approve vendor contracts involving student or staff data prior to execution.

  • Demonstrated experience with network infrastructure, device deployment, and cybersecurity
  • Experience with Google Workspace for Education administration
  • Knowledge of FERPA, COPPA, and student data privacy requirements
  • Experience supervising technology support staff across multiple sites

  • Relevant certifications (Google Certified Administrator, CompTIA Security+, or equivalent)
  • Experience with physical security systems and access control platforms
  • Familiarity with AI tools used in K-12 education
  • Experience administering a student information system in a Georgia district
  • Background in technology strategic planning and capital project management
Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities

  • Understanding of physical security technology including cameras, access control, and VoIP
  • Ability to evaluate and implement AI tools responsibly
  • Strong project management skills for deployments and capital projects

Position Summary

The Chief Financial Officer serves as the district's senior financial leader, responsible for the integrity, accuracy, and transparency of all financial operations, including the General Fund budget, special revenue and capital funds, payroll, accounts payable, purchasing, financial reporting, audit preparation, and compliance with GaDOE and state fiscal requirements.

Essential Duties and Responsibilities
Budget Development and Management
  • Lead annual development of the General Fund and all other fund budgets with the Superintendent and cabinet.

Position Summary

The Principal is the instructional leader and operational manager of the school campus, responsible for every student, every classroom, and every system within the building. In McIntosh County School System, the Principal operates within a unified leadership framework that defines clear expectations across three domains: Instruction, Intervention, and Operational Excellence. The Principal is not a middle manager who passes information between the district and the classroom. The Principal owns the instructional program, owns the results, and is accountable for what happens — and what does not happen — in every room in the building. Year One for all principals in MCSS is focused on building a reliable instructional system through five priorities: planning before teaching, teaching the planned curriculum, monitoring implementation, effective PLCs, and using data and Test Talks to adjust instruction.

Essential Duties and Responsibilities
Domain I — Instruction: Own the Instructional Program
District Commitment: Every student deserves access to a guaranteed and viable curriculum delivered through high-quality Tier 1 instruction — regardless of teacher, classroom, school, or circumstance. The Principal is responsible for ensuring this is true in every classroom, every day.
  • Own the instructional program in the building with the same clarity that the Superintendent owns the status of the district. Delegation is appropriate. Ignorance of what is happening in classrooms is not.
  • Monitor implementation, not just compliance — the goal is not to verify that plans were submitted, but to know whether what was planned is actually being taught and whether students are learning.
  • Ensure that every teacher submits the unit assessment before the unit plan, and that both are submitted together before instruction begins. The assessment defines the destination. Instruction is the path.
  • Participate with the Curriculum Director and Academic Coach in the unit plan review process, providing building-level feedback on alignment between plans, instruction, and student outcomes.
  • Conduct regular classroom walkthroughs using the four Visible Learning questions as the consistent lens: What are students learning and why? How do they know if their work is successful? What do they do when struggling? What do they do when they already understand?
  • Enforce the co-teaching standard: both teachers must be actively engaged during whole-group instruction. The special education teacher must be delivering targeted small-group intervention during the work session. A co-taught unit plan without embedded SDI is incomplete and will not be reviewed.
  • Use the Unit Planning Dashboard weekly to maintain active visibility into the instructional status of every teacher in the building.
  • Support teachers before concerns become problems. Proactive support is a leadership responsibility. Reactive response to failure is not a system.
Seven Instructional Non-Negotiables
These are not aspirational goals. These are the minimum expectations against which instructional leadership is evaluated at both the school and district level.
  • <strong>Non-Negotiable 1 — Every Student Receives a Guaranteed and Viable Curriculum:</strong> Every student has access to grade-level standards, rigorous instruction, aligned assessments, and appropriate support. A student's opportunity to learn must never depend on which teacher or classroom they are assigned to.
  • <strong>Non-Negotiable 2 — Own the Instructional Program:</strong> Know the instructional status of your school with precision. Know the status of every teacher, the strengths and weaknesses of instruction, whether interventions are working, and achievement trends by grade and subgroup.
  • <strong>Non-Negotiable 3 — Monitor What Matters:</strong> What gets monitored gets implemented. Maintain active visibility through the dashboard, classroom walkthroughs, student conversations, and PLC engagement. The key question: What was done before this became a problem?
  • <strong>Non-Negotiable 4 — Plan Before Teaching and Teach the Plan:</strong> Teachers design the unit assessment first, submit the plan and assessment together before instruction begins, address feedback before teaching starts, and teach with fidelity to the submitted plan.
  • <strong>Non-Negotiable 5 — Foster Effective Professional Learning Communities:</strong> PLCs are not meetings. They are the structure through which teachers collaborate, analyze data, problem-solve, and take collective ownership of student outcomes. The key question: Do teachers talk about my students or our students?
  • <strong>Non-Negotiable 6 — Use Data to Drive Decisions:</strong> Every Test Talk and every PLC data conversation must result in identifiable changes to instruction, intervention, or support. The key question: What changed because of the data?
  • <strong>Non-Negotiable 7 — Accountability Includes Support:</strong> The goal is to develop teachers, not simply evaluate them. Support must precede escalation. However, support without accountability is not a system. When support has been consistently provided and results have not improved, the process must move forward.
Year One Priorities
Year One Goal: The goal is not perfection. The goal is consistency. When consistent planning, implementation, collaboration, and monitoring practices are established across the building, the conditions for long-term instructional improvement are in place.
  • <strong>Priority 1 — Planning Before Teaching:</strong> Every unit assessment is designed before instruction is planned. Every unit plan and assessment is submitted together before teaching begins. Feedback is addressed before the unit starts.
  • <strong>Priority 2 — Teaching the Planned Curriculum:</strong> Teachers teach with fidelity to the submitted unit plan, using it as the working document that guides daily instruction. Minor pacing adjustments are expected. Major deviations from instructional intent are not.
  • <strong>Priority 3 — Monitoring Implementation:</strong> The Principal maintains active visibility into what is being taught through the dashboard, walkthroughs, student conversations, and instructional discussions. Monitoring is leadership, not paperwork.
  • <strong>Priority 4 — Effective PLCs Focused on Grade-Level Goals:</strong> PLCs meet regularly and focus on student learning rather than administrative tasks. Teachers speak about student success as a collective responsibility. Grade-level teams can articulate their goals, their progress, and their next steps.
  • <strong>Priority 5 — Using Data and Test Talks to Adjust Instruction:</strong> Test Talks occur consistently across the building. Every data conversation produces identifiable instructional action. Assessment results drive what happens next in the classroom.
Principal Leadership Cadence
  • <strong>Weekly:</strong> Review the Unit Planning Dashboard. Conduct classroom walkthroughs. Review PLC implementation and participation. Monitor active intervention concerns.
  • <strong>Monthly:</strong> Review school academic data. Review MTSS effectiveness and intervention rosters. Meet with building leadership team. Conduct instructional review with Academic Coach.
  • <strong>Quarterly:</strong> Analyze student growth trends. Review implementation fidelity across classrooms. Adjust school-level supports based on data.
  • <strong>Semester:</strong> Reflect on instructional progress and growth. Review overall intervention effectiveness. Set priorities for the next instructional period.
Domain II — Intervention (MTSS): No Student Falls Through the Cracks
  • Know every student receiving Tier 2 or Tier 3 support by name, need, and intervention. Be able to speak specifically and confidently about these students in any conversation with district leadership.
  • Ensure that interventions are being delivered — not just scheduled. A scheduled intervention that is not consistently occurring is not an intervention. Monitor fidelity of service delivery.
  • Ensure that progress is being monitored. Know which students are improving, which are stagnant, and which are declining despite support. Know which interventions are producing growth.
  • Ensure that interventions change when results do not. More waiting is not a strategy. Data must drive decisions. When a student is not responding, the intervention must change.
  • Lead MTSS as a principal responsibility. Interventionists, counselors, and teachers play important roles — but the principal is responsible for the system. When MTSS is working, it is because the principal has made it a priority.
Five MTSS Non-Negotiables
  • <strong>MTSS Non-Negotiable 1 — No Student Falls Through the Cracks:</strong> Every struggling student is known by name, need, and intervention. The principal can speak specifically about every student receiving support and why.
  • <strong>MTSS Non-Negotiable 2 — Interventions Must Be Delivered:</strong> Students receive the support prescribed, with fidelity, on the schedule established. Scheduling an intervention is not the same as delivering one.
  • <strong>MTSS Non-Negotiable 3 — Progress Must Be Monitored:</strong> Schools must determine whether support is working. Progress monitoring data drives decisions — not hope, habit, or the passage of time.
  • <strong>MTSS Non-Negotiable 4 — Interventions Must Change When Results Do Not:</strong> Regular data reviews occur. Honest problem-solving conversations happen. Decisions are based on evidence. When an intervention is not producing growth, something changes.
  • <strong>MTSS Non-Negotiable 5 — MTSS Is a Leadership Responsibility:</strong> The principal leads MTSS implementation, ensures interventions are occurring as scheduled, monitors student outcomes, supports intervention staff, and holds teams accountable for results.
Domain III — Operational Excellence: Enabling Conditions
  • Maintain a safe and orderly school. Students and staff must feel safe. Clear expectations, consistent discipline practices, and safe learning environments are the foundation on which all instruction rests.
  • Communicate effectively with families. Communication should be proactive, responsive, and solution-oriented. Families should not have to chase the school for information about their children.
  • Manage resources responsibly. Every dollar, every staff assignment, every facility decision should support student success. Responsible resource management is a leadership responsibility, not an administrative task.
  • Build systems that prevent problems. Strong leaders do not spend all their time reacting. Build systems that identify and address concerns before they become larger problems. A principal who is constantly in crisis mode has a systems problem.
Personnel, Accountability, and Professional Development

  • Conduct the five-stage accountability process when teachers are not meeting expectations: clarification, support, follow-up, improvement expectation, and formal accountability — in that order, with documentation at every stage.
  • Support teachers before escalating accountability. The goal is to develop teachers, not simply evaluate them. Evidence that support was provided before escalation is a district-level monitoring expectation.

  • Ensure that the campus is prepared for any GaDOE monitoring, program review, or accreditation activity, maintaining the records, documentation, and evidence required at the building level.

Position Summary

Academic Coaches are district employees whose primary accountability runs through the Curriculum Director and the district instructional system. They work alongside building principals to strengthen teaching, monitor curriculum implementation, support professional learning communities, and ensure that every student receives a guaranteed and viable curriculum through high-quality Tier 1 instruction. Academic Coaches do not evaluate teachers. They develop them. This position exists to close the gap between what is planned and what is taught, and between what is taught and what students learn.

  • Collaborate with the Curriculum Director to review every unit plan and assessment submitted by teachers before instruction begins, providing consistent and actionable feedback on rigor, depth of knowledge, standards alignment, assessment quality, and Visible Learning components.

PLC and Test Talk Facilitation

  • Lead or support Test Talk sessions in which teachers collaboratively analyze assessment data, identify students who are not responding to instruction, and develop specific instructional adjustments in response to what the data reveals.

  • Document PLC outcomes and share relevant trends with the Curriculum Director and campus principal to inform school-level instructional decisions.
  • Support grade-level teams in developing and maintaining a clear understanding of their grade-level goals, their current progress toward those goals, and the specific instructional steps they are taking in response to data.

Position Summary

A primary Year One responsibility is developing the district's MTSS manual and rolling out a systematic MTSS plan that gives every campus a clear, consistent process for identifying students, assigning interventions, monitoring progress, and adjusting support. The MTSS Coordinator reports directly to the Curriculum Director and works in close coordination with the Special Education Director, Academic Coaches, and building principals.

MTSS Manual Development and System Design
Year One Priority: Developing the district MTSS manual and rolling out a systematic, campus-wide MTSS plan is the defining responsibility of this position in 2026-2027. Everything else supports this objective.

  • Design a systematic MTSS implementation plan that provides each campus with a clear, step-by-step process for screening students, identifying those who need additional support, assigning and delivering interventions, monitoring progress through EDU Climber, and making data-driven decisions about changing or intensifying support.
  • Establish universal screening protocols and timelines for all three campuses, ensuring that screening occurs at consistent intervals across the district and that data is used to identify students needing support early rather than reactively.
  • Define clear decision rules for movement between tiers — including the data thresholds that trigger Tier 2 placement, the progress monitoring benchmarks that indicate a student is ready to exit support, and the evidence required before a student is referred for special education evaluation.
  • Develop MTSS team structures for each campus, defining membership, meeting frequency, agenda format, and documentation expectations so that school-based MTSS meetings are productive, data-driven, and consistent across all three schools.

Position Summary

The Technology Support Specialist provides day-to-day technical support, device management, and technology assistance to students, teachers, and staff at an assigned campus in McIntosh County School System. Each specialist is campus-assigned and serves as the primary on-site technology resource for their school, handling device repairs, connectivity issues, classroom technology troubleshooting, and user support in a way that minimizes disruption to instruction. While each specialist brings individual areas of technical strength and may carry district-wide responsibilities in those areas at the direction of the Technology Director, this job description defines the core expectations shared by all Technology Support Specialists. All specialists report directly to the Technology Director.

Essential Duties and Responsibilities
Device Support and Repair
  • Provide first-line technical support for all student and staff devices at the assigned campus, including Chromebooks, laptops, tablets, desktop computers, printers, and interactive display systems.
  • Diagnose and repair hardware and software issues on student and staff devices, completing repairs in a timely manner that minimizes the impact on classroom instruction and teacher productivity.
  • Manage the campus device inventory, maintaining accurate records of device assignments, condition, and repair history for all technology assets at the assigned school.
  • Process device check-in and check-out at the start and end of the school year, documenting device condition and resolving discrepancies in coordination with campus administration.
  • Escalate complex repairs, warranty claims, and issues beyond the scope of on-site repair to the Technology Director for vendor coordination or replacement.
  • Maintain a supply of spare devices and repair components at the campus level to minimize student and teacher downtime while devices are being repaired.
Classroom Technology and Infrastructure Support
  • Support the setup, configuration, and troubleshooting of classroom technology including interactive displays, projectors, document cameras, audio systems, and any other instructional technology in use at the campus.
  • Respond promptly to classroom technology failures during the instructional day, treating any issue that prevents a teacher from delivering instruction as an immediate priority.
  • Assist teachers with the technical aspects of using classroom technology tools, providing hands-on support during class setup, troubleshooting connectivity and display issues, and resolving software configuration problems.
  • Ensure that all classroom technology is functioning and ready for use at the start of each school day, conducting proactive checks on high-use equipment on a regular schedule.
  • Coordinate with the Technology Director on network connectivity issues, infrastructure upgrades, and any campus-level technology concerns that require central office involvement or vendor support.
Google Workspace and Software Support
  • Support teachers and students with Google Workspace for Education tools, including troubleshooting account access, assisting with classroom setup in Google Classroom, and resolving issues with Drive, Docs, Meet, and related applications.
  • Assist with the configuration and troubleshooting of district-approved educational software and applications used at the campus, working within the permissions and settings established by the Technology Director.
  • Help teachers troubleshoot Infinite Campus gradebook issues, attendance entry problems, and other SIS-related technical concerns, escalating system-level issues to the Technology Director or Student Information Specialist as appropriate.
  • Support student and staff account management at the campus level, including password resets, account access issues, and device enrollment in the district's mobile device management system.
Mobile Device Management and Security
  • Enroll, configure, and manage student and staff devices within the district's mobile device management (MDM) platform in accordance with settings and policies established by the Technology Director.
  • Ensure that all devices at the assigned campus are current on MDM enrollment, software updates, and security configurations, flagging unenrolled or non-compliant devices for resolution.
  • Support the enforcement of the district's acceptable use policies at the campus level, reporting patterns of misuse, circumvention of content filters, or device security concerns to the Technology Director.
  • Assist with the physical security of technology assets at the campus, including storage room organization, charging cart management, and reporting of lost, stolen, or damaged devices.
Physical Security Technology Support
  • Serve as the on-site point of contact for physical security technology at the assigned campus, including security cameras, door access control systems, intercom and entry systems, and the Centegix staff alert badge system.
  • Report any security technology malfunctions — including camera outages, door access failures, intercom issues, or badge system problems — to the Technology Director immediately, treating these as urgent safety matters.
  • Support the Technology Director in conducting regular checks of physical security technology at the campus, documenting the status of cameras, access control devices, and safety systems.
  • Assist in the onboarding of new staff at the campus with physical security technology, including key card issuance coordination, Centegix badge setup, and intercom and entry system orientation.
User Support, Training, and Communication
  • Serve as the primary technology resource and support contact for all students, teachers, and staff at the assigned campus, providing responsive, patient, and solution-oriented assistance.
  • Provide informal, just-in-time training to teachers and staff on technology tools, helping them get the most out of available resources without waiting for formal professional development.
  • Communicate clearly with campus administration about the status of open technology issues, planned maintenance, and any technology concerns that may affect instruction or operations.
  • Submit and track work orders for all significant technical issues and repairs, maintaining documentation that supports the Technology Director's oversight of campus technology operations.
  • Participate in district technology team meetings and professional development as directed by the Technology Director, contributing campus-level knowledge and collaborating with fellow specialists on shared challenges.

Finance Specialist

Current incumbent: Currently: Carly Rayhon
FinanceFull-TimeCentral Office
Reporting Structure
Reports directly to: Chief Financial Officer
Direct Reports & Areas of Oversight
Position Summary

The Finance Specialist is responsible for the preparation, calculation, and processing of payroll for all employees of McIntosh County School System. This role manages the full payroll preparation cycle — from timesheet collection and verification through salary calculation, supplement processing, deduction management, and benefits coordination — ensuring that every employee is paid accurately and on time. The Finance Specialist works in close coordination with the Chief Financial Officer, who executes the final payroll run and provides oversight of all payroll disbursements. In a small district, this position carries significant responsibility for the accuracy of one of the district's largest and most sensitive financial functions. This position reports directly to the Chief Financial Officer.

Essential Duties and Responsibilities
Payroll Preparation and Processing
Core Responsibility: The Finance Specialist owns the payroll preparation process from start to finish. Every payroll cycle begins with this position and is reviewed and executed by the CFO. Accuracy and timeliness are non-negotiable — every employee depends on this function.
  • Collect, review, and verify timesheets and leave records for all district employees each pay period, ensuring that hours worked, leave taken, and substitute coverage are accurately documented before payroll is processed.
  • Calculate employee salaries and wages for each pay period in accordance with board-approved salary schedules, individual contracts, and applicable Georgia educator compensation requirements.
  • Determine and apply accurate years-of-service credit for all employees, ensuring that salary placement on the district's salary schedule reflects verified experience and that any changes in placement are documented and approved.
  • Process salary schedule lane changes, degree upgrades, and additional certification supplements when employees meet eligibility requirements, coordinating with Human Resources to verify documentation before adjusting compensation.
  • Calculate and apply all employee supplements including coaching supplements, extracurricular supplements, and any other board-approved additional compensation, verifying eligibility and board approval before processing.
  • Prepare the complete payroll register for each pay cycle and submit it to the Chief Financial Officer for review and final execution, ensuring that all calculations are accurate, all deductions are correct, and all supporting documentation is complete.
Salary Schedule Administration
  • Maintain the district's master salary schedule and individual employee compensation records, ensuring that each employee's placement accurately reflects their education level, years of experience, and any board-approved adjustments.
  • Process new hire salary placements in coordination with Human Resources, verifying experience documentation, degree verification, and certification status before establishing initial salary placement.
  • Apply annual step increases and any board-approved across-the-board salary adjustments at the start of each fiscal year, updating employee records and verifying that salary schedule changes are reflected accurately in the payroll system.
  • Maintain accurate records of all salary placement decisions, lane changes, supplement assignments, and compensation history for every district employee.
  • Calculate salary projections and staffing cost estimates as requested by the CFO to support budget development, position control, and financial planning.
Deductions, Benefits, and Retirement
  • Manage all payroll deductions for each employee including Georgia Teachers Retirement System (TRS) contributions, State Health Benefit Plan (SHBP) premiums, flexible spending accounts, voluntary deductions, and any court-ordered garnishments.
  • Ensure that TRS contributions are calculated correctly for each employee classification, that contribution rates reflect current TRS requirements, and that monthly TRS reports and remittances are prepared accurately and on time.
  • Coordinate SHBP premium deductions with enrollment changes, ensuring that coverage elections, dependent additions, and terminations are reflected accurately in payroll deductions within required timelines.
  • Process leave without pay (LWOP) deductions accurately, calculating the correct daily rate and adjusting salary and benefit deductions as required for the period of unpaid leave.
  • Ensure that all voluntary deductions — including supplemental insurance, tax-sheltered annuities, and other employee-elected withholdings — are processed accurately each pay period in accordance with employee authorization forms.
Health Insurance and Employee Benefits Coordination
  • Serve as the district's primary point of contact for the State Health Benefit Plan (SHBP), coordinating all employee enrollment, plan changes, dependent additions, and terminations in the SHBP system within required deadlines.
  • Assist employees with SHBP enrollment during new hire onboarding, annual open enrollment periods, and qualifying life events, explaining available plan options and ensuring that election forms are completed and submitted accurately.
  • Process SHBP qualifying life event changes — including marriage, birth, adoption, divorce, and loss of other coverage — within the plan's required 30-day enrollment window, coordinating with the employee and SHBP to ensure timely coverage changes.
  • Maintain accurate SHBP enrollment records for all active employees, retirees continuing coverage, and COBRA participants, reconciling the district's enrollment roster against SHBP billing statements each month and resolving any discrepancies.
  • Manage COBRA administration for employees separating from the district, ensuring that required COBRA election notices are sent within federally mandated timelines and that coverage continuation records are maintained accurately.
  • Coordinate enrollment and administration of any supplemental or voluntary benefit programs offered by the district, including supplemental life insurance, dental, vision, cancer, and disability coverage offered through third-party carriers.
  • Serve as the liaison between employees and benefit carriers, helping staff navigate coverage questions, claims issues, and plan changes, and escalating unresolved issues to the appropriate carrier representative or the CFO.
  • Ensure that all benefit-related deductions are reconciled to carrier invoices each month and that the district is not paying premiums for employees who are no longer enrolled or eligible.
  • Track and administer FMLA and extended leave coordination as it intersects with benefit continuation, notifying employees of their rights and obligations regarding premium payments during leave periods.
  • Support the CFO in benefits cost analysis, open enrollment planning, and any communications to employees regarding changes to benefit offerings or plan costs.
Tax Reporting and Compliance
  • Ensure that all federal and state payroll tax withholdings are calculated correctly for each employee each pay period, including federal income tax, Social Security (if applicable), Medicare, and Georgia income tax.
  • Prepare required payroll tax remittances and ensure that federal tax deposits are submitted to the IRS on the district's required deposit schedule.
  • Prepare and submit quarterly payroll tax reports including IRS Form 941 and Georgia Department of Revenue withholding reports within required filing deadlines.
  • Prepare annual W-2 forms for all district employees, ensuring that wages, withholdings, and benefit information are accurately reported and that W-2s are distributed to employees within IRS-required timelines.
  • Maintain payroll records in compliance with IRS record retention requirements and Georgia public records retention schedules.
Coordination with HR and Finance
  • Work closely with the Human Resources office to ensure that new hire information, terminations, position changes, and leave status changes are reflected accurately in payroll records before each pay cycle.
  • Coordinate with the CFO on payroll budget monitoring, ensuring that actual salary expenditures are tracked against budget appropriations and that any significant variances are identified and reported promptly.
  • Support the annual independent financial audit by preparing payroll documentation, salary schedule verification, and employee compensation records as requested by external auditors.
  • Assist the CFO in preparing year-end payroll close documentation, W-2 reconciliation, and any required state or federal payroll reporting beyond the regular quarterly cycle.
  • Maintain strict confidentiality of all employee compensation information, tax records, and payroll data, handling sensitive personnel financial information with the discretion required of a trusted finance position.

  • Minimum two years of experience in payroll processing, accounting, or a related financial role
  • Demonstrated proficiency with payroll software, financial management systems, and standard office productivity applications
  • Strong mathematical ability and attention to detail in salary calculation, deduction processing, and tax withholding
  • Familiarity with federal and Georgia payroll tax requirements, W-2 reporting, and IRS deposit schedules
  • Ability to handle confidential employee compensation information with discretion and maintain strict data security practices

  • Experience processing payroll in a Georgia public school district, including familiarity with TRS contribution requirements and SHBP premium administration
  • Knowledge of Georgia educator salary schedules, years-of-service credit calculation, and certification-based lane placement
  • Experience with IRS Form 941 preparation, annual W-2 processing, and payroll tax deposit compliance
  • Familiarity with leave management, LWOP calculations, and the interaction between leave records and payroll deductions
  • Background supporting payroll-related audit documentation and annual financial close processes
  • Certified Payroll Professional (CPP) credential or equivalent professional certification
Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities
  • Strong payroll calculation skills including salary schedule placement, years-of-service determination, supplement calculation, and deduction processing
  • Thorough knowledge of federal and Georgia payroll tax withholding requirements, deposit schedules, and annual reporting obligations
  • Understanding of Georgia TRS contribution requirements, SHBP premium administration, and the benefit deduction implications of employment status changes
  • Ability to manage a complete payroll cycle accurately and on schedule for an entire school district workforce
  • Exceptional attention to detail and commitment to accuracy in a function where errors directly affect employee livelihoods

Position Summary

Tina Thomas serves in a dual-role administrative position split between the Special Education department and the Technology department. In her Special Education capacity under Dr. Rachel Carlyle, she functions as the department's secretary — organizing and maintaining special education files, tracking IEP and evaluation meeting timelines, sending required meeting notices, coordinating communications on behalf of the School Psychologist, and providing general administrative support to the Special Education Director. In her Technology capacity under Rusty Boone, she processes purchase orders in the district's financial system and provides general administrative support as assigned by the Technology Director. This position requires strong organizational skills, the ability to manage compliance-sensitive timelines, and the discretion to handle confidential student records and departmental information in both roles.

Essential Duties and Responsibilities
Special Education — Records and File Management
Special Education Function: The following duties are performed under the direction of the Special Education Director.
  • Organize and maintain all special education student files, ensuring that records are complete, accurately labeled, properly stored, and accessible to authorized personnel in compliance with FERPA and applicable Georgia student records requirements.
  • Establish and maintain a consistent filing system for IEP documents, evaluation reports, eligibility determinations, consent forms, progress monitoring records, and all other required special education documentation across all three campuses.
  • Ensure that student records are kept confidential, that only authorized individuals have access to special education files, and that records requests are processed in accordance with FERPA and district policy.
  • Support the transition of records when students enroll, transfer, or exit the district, coordinating with receiving schools and the Special Education Director to ensure that records are transferred completely and on time.
  • Assist the Special Education Director in maintaining accurate records of active IEPs, re-evaluation due dates, annual review schedules, and eligibility documentation for the district's full special education caseload.
Special Education — Timeline Tracking and Meeting Coordination
  • Maintain a comprehensive timeline tracking system for all required special education meetings and deadlines across all three campuses, including initial eligibility conferences, annual IEP reviews, triennial re-evaluations, and any other procedurally required meeting dates.
  • Monitor upcoming IEP and evaluation deadlines on a rolling basis, alerting the Special Education Director and School Psychologist well in advance of approaching timelines so that no federally required meeting is missed or delayed.
  • Send all required meeting notices to parents, guardians, and team members for IEP meetings, eligibility conferences, and re-evaluation meetings, ensuring that notices are sent within required timelines and include all information required by IDEA procedural safeguards.
  • Coordinate scheduling for special education meetings, working with the Special Education Director, School Psychologist, campus principals, teachers, and families to identify meeting times and arrange logistics.
  • Maintain documentation of all meeting notices sent, including dates mailed or delivered, confirmation of receipt where applicable, and records of any rescheduling or family communication related to meeting coordination.
  • Track and document parent consent forms for initial evaluations, re-evaluations, and IEP services, alerting the Special Education Director when consents are outstanding or when timeline clocks have started.
Special Education — Communications and Administrative Support
  • Manage outgoing communications on behalf of the Special Education Director and School Psychologist, including drafting and sending correspondence to families, schools, outside agencies, and GaDOE as directed.
  • Coordinate communications from the School Psychologist to families regarding evaluation appointments, consent for evaluation, results conferences, and follow-up scheduling.
  • Answer incoming calls and correspondence directed to the Special Education department, routing inquiries to the appropriate staff member and providing accurate information on routine matters within the scope of this position.
  • Prepare and organize materials needed for IEP meetings, eligibility conferences, and re-evaluation meetings, including printing forms, compiling documentation, and ensuring that meeting packets are complete and ready in advance.
  • Support the Special Education Director with general departmental administrative tasks including scheduling, correspondence, copying, scanning, and document preparation as assigned.
  • Assist with the preparation of required GaDOE special education reports and data submissions as directed by the Special Education Director, organizing documentation and compiling records needed for state reporting.
Technology — Purchase Order Processing and Administrative Support
Technology Function: The following duties are performed under the direction of the Technology Director.
  • Process purchase orders for the Technology department, entering PO information accurately into the district's financial system in accordance with the Technology Director's direction and the district's purchasing procedures.
  • Maintain organized records of all Technology department purchase orders, including vendor information, item descriptions, amounts, and approval status, ensuring that PO documentation is complete and filed appropriately.
  • Coordinate with vendors on Technology department purchases as directed by the Technology Director, communicating order details, following up on delivery status, and routing vendor correspondence to the appropriate staff member.
  • Support the Technology Director with general administrative tasks including scheduling, correspondence, document preparation, filing, and other clerical functions assigned to this position.
  • Assist with the organization and tracking of technology procurement and purchasing records to support the Technology Director's budget monitoring and CFO reporting requirements.
Dual-Role Responsibilities and Confidentiality
  • Manage time and workload effectively across two supervisory relationships, communicating proactively with both the Special Education Director and the Technology Director when scheduling conflicts or competing priorities require coordination.
  • Maintain absolute confidentiality of all special education student records, IEP documentation, evaluation data, and any other personally identifiable information encountered in the Special Education half of this role.
  • Handle Technology department purchasing records and financial documentation with the same discretion and accuracy required of all district financial support functions.
  • Exercise sound professional judgment about which supervisor to consult when situations arise outside routine responsibilities, and seek guidance before acting on matters that require authorization.

  • Strong organizational skills with demonstrated ability to manage multiple filing systems, deadline calendars, and concurrent responsibilities
  • Proficiency with standard office software and the ability to learn district-specific systems including the special education records platform and financial management software
  • Excellent written communication skills for drafting professional correspondence, meeting notices, and departmental communications
  • Demonstrated ability to handle sensitive and confidential information including student records and personnel documents with discretion
Preferred

  • Experience processing purchase orders or providing financial administrative support in a school district or government setting
  • Familiarity with Georgia special education regulations, IEP meeting notice requirements, and FERPA obligations for student records
  • Background in coordinating meeting schedules, maintaining compliance calendars, and managing deadline-sensitive documentation
Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities
  • Strong organizational ability to maintain accurate, complete, and confidential special education files and compliance timelines across a full district caseload
  • Skill in drafting clear and professional meeting notices, parent correspondence, and departmental communications on behalf of the Special Education Director and School Psychologist
  • Accuracy and attention to detail in purchase order processing, financial record maintenance, and administrative documentation
  • Ability to track multiple concurrent IEP, evaluation, and re-evaluation deadlines without allowing any to lapse
  • Professionalism and discretion in handling confidential student records, family communications, and departmental information in two sensitive departments simultaneously
  • Flexibility in managing two supervisory relationships and shifting between Special Education and Technology responsibilities as the workday requires
  • Clear and courteous communication with families, staff, vendors, and outside agencies on behalf of both departments
  • Dependability and follow-through in a role where two departments rely on this position to keep administrative functions running accurately and on time
Working Conditions
  • Full-time position split between the Special Education department and the Technology department at the central office, with regular presence at all three campuses as needed to support meeting coordination and records functions.
  • Special Education timeline deadlines and IEP meeting cycles create recurring peaks in the compliance calendar throughout the school year.
  • Physical demands are consistent with a typical school and office environment, including document scanning, filing, and extended computer use.
  • Performance evaluated jointly by the Special Education Director and the Technology Director using district evaluation criteria.
